## What We Did

During this week's team work, the previous week's UrbanStyle dashboards were refined into data stories. The focus was no longer only on creating charts, but on making each view quickly explain to a specific manager what is happening, why it matters, and what should be done next.

The team divided the work by locations and channels. Each member created one view: Tallinn, Tartu, Pärnu, or the online store. In the end, the individual views were combined into a summary story that helps Anna and management compare the results of UrbanStyle's different locations.

## Team Work Focus

The main goal of week 6 was to make visualizations clearer and more decision-supporting. To do this, we added executive summaries, annotations, reference lines, and short business narratives to the dashboards.

We used the data storytelling framework:

- setup;
- conflict or question;
- data;
- interpretation;
- recommendation or action.

We also used the "So what?" test so that every important number would not remain only descriptive, but would lead to business meaning.

## Roles and Tasks

- Role A: Tallinn store dashboard and narrative. The focus was on the strengths of the largest store and what other locations can learn from Tallinn.
- Role B: Tartu store dashboard and narrative. The focus was on finding a possible downward trend, problematic periods, and improvement opportunities.
- Role C: Pärnu store dashboard and narrative. The focus was on seasonality, the impact of summer months, and risks in the winter period.
- Role D: online store dashboard and narrative. The focus was on online channel growth, scalability, and the potential of digital sales.

## Work Process

- We read Anna's challenge and discussed why each location needs a separate view.
- We divided roles between Tallinn, Tartu, Pärnu, and the online store.
- Each participant filtered the data in Power BI by their location or channel.
- For each view, 3-5 visuals were created, including KPI cards, sales trend, and product or category comparison.
- At least two annotations were added to explain important changes or patterns.
- At least one reference line was added, such as a target or average monthly revenue.
- Each member wrote a 3-4 sentence data story for their dashboard.
- The team compared results and created a shared summary conclusion for Anna.

## Results

As a result of the team work, location-based dashboards were created that show UrbanStyle's different sales locations from separate perspectives. This division helps better understand that different locations inside the same company can behave very differently.

The Tallinn view helps show the patterns of the strongest physical store. The Tartu view helps notice possible problems and improvement areas. The Pärnu view highlights the impact of seasonality. The online store view shows the growth potential of the digital channel.

The team's shared conclusion was that UrbanStyle's management should not evaluate all locations by the same standard. Each location needs its own strategy: Tallinn should maintain its strengths, Tartu should investigate the reasons for decline, Pärnu should plan for seasonality, and the online store should grow scalable sales.

## Summary Story

UrbanStyle's locations show that the company has several different growth stories at the same time: Tallinn is the strong main sales location, Tartu needs closer attention, Pärnu depends on seasonality, and the online store creates an opportunity to grow without new physical stores.

The recommendation for Anna is to manage each location with a separate data-based plan, not to use the same campaign or inventory decision for all stores.

## AI Usage

AI helped formulate data stories and check whether the conclusions passed the "So what?" test. We also used AI to create drafts for annotations and executive summaries, but the final conclusions were checked against the Power BI visuals.

## Main Lessons Learned

- The same dataset can tell a different story depending on whether we look at Tallinn, Tartu, Pärnu, or the online store.
- A good dashboard must answer a business question, not only show numbers.
- Annotations help the viewer understand more quickly what to notice in the chart.
- A reference line gives context: whether the result is above or below expectation.
- A data story turns a dashboard into a decision-supporting tool.
- Location-based analysis helps management make more precise strategic decisions.

## Summary

As a result of the team work, we learned how to turn Power BI dashboards into clearer data stories. We practiced refining visualizations, adding annotations, using reference lines, and writing business interpretation. The most important lesson was that a dashboard is only finished when the viewer understands what the data means and which decision it supports.
