# Week 1 Team Work: SQL Basics

Eestikeelne versioon: [README.md](README.md)

## Summary

We practiced exploring UrbanStyle's main tables with SQL. The focus was on simple `SELECT`, `WHERE`, `ORDER BY`, `LIMIT`, and aggregation-preparation queries to understand sales, customer, product, and channel-based data.

## Role Distribution

| Role | Team Member | Task |
|------|-------------|------|
| A | Ragnar | Sales transactions: explored amounts, dates, and basic sales patterns in the `sales` table. |
| B | Karmo | Customer data: mapped the fields, cities, and customer segment information in the `customers` table. |
| C | Mari | Product data: explored categories, prices, and the product portfolio in the `products` table. |
| D | Kätlin | Sales channels: analyzed the distribution of channels and locations in the `sales` table. |

## Role Descriptions

- Role A, the sales transaction analyst, focused on the `sales` table. The task was to find which fields describe sales amounts, dates, and orders, and what first questions can be asked based on them.
- Role B, the customer data analyst, focused on the `customers` table. The task was to understand customer attributes, the distribution of cities and segments, and explain how customer information supports sales analysis.
- Role C, the product data analyst, focused on the `products` table. The task was to describe categories, prices, and the product portfolio so that sales could later be connected with products.
- Role D, the channel and location analyst, focused on sales channels and where sales happened. The task was to separate online and physical sales views and write the first channel-based observations.

## Main Skills

- Browsing SQL tables and explaining the meaning of columns.
- Using filters and sorting to answer business questions.
- Writing first observations in language that is understandable for Anna and Toomas.

## Output

The team created an initial overview of what UrbanStyle data exists and what questions can be explored more deeply in the following weeks.
