## What We Did

During this week's team work, we applied visualization design principles to solve UrbanStyle's business problem. The goal was to create dashboards suitable for different stakeholders, because Kristi, Anna, and Liis need different views from the same data.

As a result of the work, we created visual views that help answer three main questions: whether UrbanStyle is growing, which channels and categories bring results, and whether the company looks attractive to an investor.

## Team Work Focus

We worked with UrbanStyle's sales, customer, product, and inventory data. The focus was not only on making charts, but on ensuring that every visualization answered a specific business question and was quickly understandable for the target audience.

We used the principles learned during the session: choosing the right chart type, using KPI cards, creating clear visual hierarchy, reducing unnecessary noise, and adding business interpretation.

## Roles and Tasks

- Role A: CEO view for Kristi, showing high-level KPIs, revenue trend, and yearly growth.
- Role B: marketing view for Anna, comparing sales channel performance and understanding which channels bring more sales or customers.
- Role C: operations view for Liis, looking at sales by store and connecting them with inventory and categories.
- Role D: investor summary view, bringing together the main findings from A, B, and C into one understandable overview.

## Work Process

- We read through Kristi's, Anna's, and Liis's challenges and discussed why different roles need different dashboards.
- We divided roles in the team based on the stakeholders.
- Each participant selected suitable metrics and chart types for their own view.
- We created the dashboards and made sure that titles, labels, colors, and layout supported understanding the message.
- We added a short business interpretation to each view so that the chart would not remain only a picture, but would help support a decision.
- We summarized the main results in the investor view and formulated a shared recommendation for Kristi.

## Results

Based on the team's work, we concluded that UrbanStyle's revenue in 2024 grew by 19.08% compared with 2023. This shows positive yearly growth and supports good growth potential from an investor's perspective.

The strongest product categories were shoes, men's clothing, and women's clothing. Across channels and stores, Tallinn and Online sales stood out as the most important. This means that UrbanStyle should keep its focus on strong core categories while also growing the potential of the online channel.

## Main Lessons Learned

- The same dataset may need a completely different presentation depending on whether the viewer is a CEO, marketing manager, operations manager, or investor.
- A good dashboard starts with a business question, not with a chart type.
- KPI cards work well for management and investors because they give a quick overview of the situation.
- A line chart works well for showing trends, a bar chart for comparisons, and pie charts should be used carefully so they do not become too busy.
- The value of a visualization increases when a clear "what does this mean?" conclusion is added to it.
- In design, it is important to remove unnecessary noise so that the viewer immediately sees the most important information.
