# Week 5: Visualization Design

Eestikeelne versioon: [README.md](README.md)

This week I learned how to turn data into a visual story that is easy to understand and helps people make decisions faster. I understood that a good dashboard is not just a collection of charts, but a carefully designed tool that answers specific business questions.

First, I learned how to choose the right chart type based on the question. If I want to show change over time, a line chart works well. If I want to compare categories, a bar chart is a better choice. A KPI card helps highlight one important number, such as revenue or growth. I understood that the choice of chart directly affects how quickly the viewer understands the message.

Second, I learned the importance of dashboard layout and visual hierarchy. I understood that the most important metrics should be visible first, and the viewer's eye should move logically from the overview to the details. The principles of the Z-pattern and F-pattern helped me think about how a manager or investor actually reads a dashboard.

Third, I learned how to plan filters and interactivity. I understood that a good dashboard should not answer only one static question, but should allow the user to view the data by city, category, period, or channel. Filters make the analysis more flexible and help different users find the view they need.

Through the UrbanStyle example, I learned how to connect visualization with a business goal. When planning Kristi's investor dashboard, I thought about the questions an investor would most likely ask: whether the company is growing, which categories sell best, which channels bring results, and where the risks may be. This helped me see that visualization should support decision-making, not just make data look nicer.

In summary, this week I learned to:

- choose the chart type based on the business question;
- distinguish when to use a line chart, bar chart, pie chart, scatter plot, or KPI card;
- plan a logical dashboard layout;
- use visual hierarchy so that the most important information is visible immediately;
- plan filters and interactivity;
- think of a dashboard as a decision-making tool, not just a report.

This week I understood that data visualization is a very important skill in an analyst's work, because even correct numbers can become useless if they are not presented clearly. Good visualization helps turn a complex dataset into a clear story that supports better business decisions.
