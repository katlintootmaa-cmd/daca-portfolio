# Week 9: Career Preparation and Employer Perspective

Estonian version: [README.md](README.md)

## Weekly Focus

Week 9 focused on career preparation and on how an employer evaluates a data analyst candidate. Instead of looking only at our own CV or LinkedIn profile, we analyzed the employer's perspective: what UrbanStyle's managers would look for in a new data analyst and how they would evaluate candidates.

The main practical task was to create an UrbanStyle data analyst hiring guide. The team had 4 members, and the work was divided by roles: hiring manager view, technical interviewer view, HR manager view, and team lead view.

## What I Learned During the Week

I learned that for an employer, it is important to see evidence of skills, not only a list of skills. SQL, Python, Power BI, or GitHub are stronger when they are connected to a concrete project and result. For example, it is much more convincing to write that I analyzed 10,000+ sales records and identified a VIP customer segment than to simply say that I know SQL.

I also learned that the portfolio is one of the most important pieces of evidence for a data analyst. A good portfolio does not contain only code, but also an explanation: what the problem was, how it was solved, which tools were used, and what decision or business value was reached.

For interviews, the STAR method was important to me. It helps answer questions in a structured way: situation, task, action, and result. In a data analyst interview, it is especially important to express the result in numbers, because that is easier for the employer to remember.

## Team Work Result

As team work, we created an UrbanStyle hiring guide divided into six chapters:

1. Technical skills evaluation
2. Portfolio evaluation
3. CV and LinkedIn evaluation
4. 30-60-90 day onboarding plan
5. Writing a job offer
6. Interview scenario

All chapters are saved as separate files in the `week-09/team` folder.

## My Main Conclusions

A good junior data analyst does not need to be an expert immediately, but must show learning ability, logical thinking, and the ability to place data in a business context.

The CV and LinkedIn should be concrete and result-based. General expressions do not distinguish a candidate, but measurable results and clear projects do.

Portfolio README files are very important because an employer may not immediately read the code deeply. A clear project description helps them quickly understand what the candidate can do.
