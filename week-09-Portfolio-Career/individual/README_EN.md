# Individual Work: Week 9 Career Preparation

Estonian version: [README.md](README.md)

## Task Goal

The goal of the Week 9 individual work was to think through how I can better show my data analyst skills to an employer. The focus was not only on writing a CV, but on the full job-market readiness package: CV, LinkedIn, GitHub portfolio, interview preparation, and connecting my earlier experience with the data analyst role.

## What I Learned

I understood that an employer is not only looking for a list of tools. If I write SQL, Python, and Power BI in my CV, that alone is not enough. I need to also show where I used them and what result came from that work. For example, in the UrbanStyle projects, I can highlight sales data analysis, customer segmentation, dashboards, and automation.

An important learning point was also that results should be as concrete as possible. A general sentence like "worked with data" does not show much. It is much stronger to say that I analyzed sales records, found valuable customer segments, or created a dashboard that helps managers make decisions.

## What I Learned About the CV

The CV must be short, clear, and adapted to the specific job posting. In a junior data analyst CV, technical skills, projects, and measurable results should be clearly visible. As a career changer, I do not need to hide my previous experience, but show how it gives me business understanding.

My next step with the CV is to add more concrete UrbanStyle project results and reduce overly general descriptions.

## What I Learned About LinkedIn

LinkedIn is not only an online CV, but a place where an employer or recruiter can quickly understand who I am and what I am looking for. The headline should include the role, skills, and value proposition. For example, a suitable direction is "Junior Data Analyst | SQL, Python, Power BI".

In the About section, I should tell my career-change story: why I am moving toward data analysis, what I have learned, and what value I can offer an employer. In the Featured section, I should add my best GitHub projects.

## What I Learned About the Portfolio

The portfolio is my strongest evidence. It shows that I have not only learned theory, but have actually worked with data. Every GitHub project should be readable even for someone who does not know my full learning journey.

For this reason, every project README should answer at least these questions:

- what was the background of the problem
- what data I used
- what tools I used
- what I analyzed
- what result or conclusion I reached

## What I Learned About Interviews

For interview preparation, the STAR method is important. It helps answer questions in a structured way: situation, task, action, and result. For me, it is especially important to practice how to talk about UrbanStyle projects briefly and concretely.

I need to prepare at least 2-3 examples:

- one about solving a technical problem
- one about teamwork
- one about a situation where analysis helped make a better decision

## My Main Conclusions

The most important conclusion from this individual work is that in job search, my story must be clear. I do not need to present myself as someone who knows everything, but as an entry-level data analyst with practical projects, learning ability, and previous experience that can be connected to data work.

I also understood that an employer looks at a candidate from several angles: technical skills, portfolio, CV, LinkedIn, communication skills, and business thinking. Therefore, all my materials must tell the same story.

## Next Steps

- organize the README files of my GitHub projects
- improve the CV with concrete results and numbers
- update the LinkedIn headline and About section
- add the best projects to the LinkedIn Featured section
- prepare STAR answers for interviews
- practice a 3-5 minute portfolio introduction

## Summary

Week 9 helped me understand how to move from the learner role to the job seeker role. I already have practical projects and skills, but now I need to make them more visible. The most important thing is to show an employer not only what I learned, but also what value I can create with data.
