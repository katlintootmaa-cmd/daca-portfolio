# Hiring Guide for the Role: Product Analyst

Estonian version: [README.md](README.md)

## Role Focus

A Product Analyst helps UrbanStyle understand how customers behave with products, the online store, campaigns, or features. The role focuses on user behavior, product decisions, experimentation, and metrics.

## How to Use This Guide

This guide can be used throughout the Product Analyst hiring process: writing the job posting, reviewing CV and LinkedIn, evaluating the portfolio, preparing a product case, conducting the interview, and making the final decision.

For each candidate, three main questions should be evaluated:

- whether the candidate understands user behavior and product metrics;
- whether they can turn data into a product hypothesis or experiment;
- whether they can explain recommendations to a product manager or business team.

Recommended decision:

- **Move to the next round** if the candidate can connect metrics with a product decision and suggest a testable next step.
- **Needs additional checking** if the candidate understands metrics but cannot yet form a clear hypothesis or recommendation.
- **Not suitable at the moment** if the candidate describes numbers without connecting them to product behavior or decisions.

## Role View and Work

In this group work, we looked at the Product Analyst role from four perspectives. The hiring manager evaluated how product analytics appears in the CV, LinkedIn, and job posting. The technical interviewer focused on SQL, event data, funnel analysis, conversion, retention, and churn. The HR manager evaluated communication with cross-functional teams. The team lead looked at whether recommendations are testable, practical, and connected to product priorities.

We used the same four views in every chapter so that the guide would evaluate metrics, hypotheses, technical skill, and collaboration together.

## 1. Technical Skills Evaluation

In a Product Analyst role, the candidate is expected to analyze user behavior: conversion, purchase journey, product category views, adding to cart, and completing a purchase. SQL is important, and the candidate must also understand metrics such as conversion rate, retention, and churn.

The employer checks whether the candidate can connect data with a product decision.

Group result: the technical task could be a funnel or purchase journey case where the candidate identifies where users drop off and suggests a next step.

**Examples: what is reviewed and asked**

- Reviewed: whether the candidate names the right events, calculates conversion, identifies drop-off points, and connects the finding to a product hypothesis.
- Asked: "Where in the funnel would you look first?", "Which metric would show whether the change works?", "How would you test your recommendation?"

## 2. Portfolio Evaluation

A Product Analyst portfolio should include a project about user behavior, funnel analysis, retention, A/B testing, or product category performance.

The employer checks whether the candidate can move from a metric to a hypothesis and then to a measurable product recommendation.

Group result: a strong portfolio includes a clear product question, analysis, hypothesis, and recommended next step.

**Examples: what is reviewed and asked**

- Reviewed: whether the project has a product problem, user behavior data, metrics, hypothesis, and recommendation.
- Asked: "What product decision did this analysis support?", "Which user behavior did you investigate?", "How would you measure whether the recommendation worked?"

## 3. CV and LinkedIn Evaluation

The CV should highlight product metrics, funnel analysis, retention, experimentation, SQL, dashboards, and business impact. Previous experience in product, e-commerce, UX, or marketing can be useful.

The LinkedIn headline could be: "Product Analyst | SQL, Funnel Analytics, Retention | Turning User Behavior into Product Decisions".

Group result: the profile should show that the candidate understands users, metrics, and product decisions.

**Examples: what is reviewed and asked**

- Reviewed: whether the CV includes product metrics, user behavior analysis, experiments, tools, and concrete outcomes.
- Asked: "Which product metric do you care about most?", "How would you analyze a drop in conversion?", "How does your previous experience help in product analytics?"

## 4. 30-60-90 Day Onboarding Plan

First 30 days: learn UrbanStyle's customer journey, product catalog, online store data, and key product metrics.

Days 30-60: analyze one funnel or product category and identify a possible improvement area.

Days 60-90: create one product recommendation or experiment plan with success metrics.

Group result: a Product Analyst must learn how customers behave and how product decisions are measured.

**Examples: what is reviewed and asked**

- Reviewed: whether the plan connects product metrics, user behavior, funnel analysis, and one measurable recommendation.
- Asked: "Which part of the purchase journey would you study first?", "What would be a good first experiment?", "How would you measure product improvement?"

## 5. Writing a Job Offer

The job posting should emphasize user behavior analysis, product metrics, funnel or retention analysis, experimentation, and collaboration with product, marketing, and engineering teams.

Requirements: SQL, analytics thinking, dashboarding, understanding of product metrics, and clear communication.

Group result: the posting should speak to candidates who can connect data with product decisions and testable hypotheses.

**Examples: what is reviewed and asked**

- Reviewed: whether the posting explains product analytics, metrics, experiments, stakeholders, and the expected portfolio.
- Asked: "Does the posting clearly describe product decisions?", "Are metrics named specifically?", "Does it invite candidates with e-commerce or product experience?"

## 6. Interview Scenario

The interview could include a case: "Many customers view products, but fewer than expected add them to cart. How would you investigate the problem?"

The candidate should think about funnel steps, product pages, category differences, user segments, and possible experiments.

Group result: the Product Analyst interview must evaluate product metrics, hypothesis thinking, and measurable recommendations.

**Examples: what is reviewed and asked**

- Reviewed: whether the candidate moves from metric to hypothesis and from hypothesis to a measurable next step.
- Asked: "Which events would you need?", "How would you distinguish a UX problem from a product selection problem?", "What experiment would you suggest?"

**More Detailed Interview Structure**

- 5 minutes: introduction and candidate background.
- 15 minutes: portfolio or product analysis walk-through.
- 15 minutes: practical funnel or product behavior case.
- 15 minutes: STAR questions about collaboration with product, design, marketing, or engineering.
- 5 minutes: candidate questions.
- 5 minutes: next steps.

**Interview Evaluation Criteria**

- Product metrics: conversion, funnel, retention, churn, and experiment logic.
- Technical skill: SQL, event data, user journey analysis, and result validation.
- Business thinking: whether the recommendation supports a product decision.
- Collaboration and learning ability: whether the candidate can work with a product manager and prioritize by impact.

## Strong vs Weak Candidate

## Evaluation Scale Summary

| Area | 1 - needs development | 2 - suitable level | 3 - strong level |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product metrics | Does not understand funnel or retention logic | Chooses suitable core metrics | Connects metrics with product health and decision |
| Hypotheses | Does not offer a testable assumption | Offers one realistic hypothesis | Connects the hypothesis with an experiment or measurement |
| Technical skill | Cannot name needed events | Understands SQL and event data | Finds a user journey problem in the data |
| Collaboration | Does not consider product team needs | Explains recommendation to a product manager | Prioritizes recommendation by impact and feasibility |

The candidate is a strong recommendation if they can move from a metric to a hypothesis and from there to a measurable product recommendation in the case task.

**Strong candidate:** understands the user journey, can choose product metrics, and connects analysis with a concrete product decision. They form hypotheses, use funnel or event data, and offer testable next steps. The portfolio includes, for example, purchase journey, retention, or A/B test analysis with a clear recommendation for a product manager.

**Weak candidate:** describes metrics without a product hypothesis, does not name needed events, and cannot explain what decision the analysis supports. Their recommendations are vague and not measurable.
