# Hiring Guide for the Role: Risk Analyst

Estonian version: [README.md](README.md)

## Role Focus

A Risk Analyst helps UrbanStyle notice and reduce business risks. These may be related to inventory, payments, returns, fraud, deliveries, data quality, or financial impact. The role requires precision, critical thinking, and the ability to find unusual patterns.

## How to Use This Guide

This guide can be used throughout the Risk Analyst hiring process: writing the job posting, reviewing CV and LinkedIn, evaluating the portfolio, preparing a risk case, conducting the interview, and making the final decision.

For each candidate, three main questions should be evaluated:

- whether the candidate checks data quality before making conclusions;
- whether they can separate a data error, seasonality, and a real business risk;
- whether they can offer a practical control or preventive action.

Recommended decision:

- **Move to the next round** if the candidate finds anomalies in the dataset, checks causes, and evaluates risk impact.
- **Needs additional checking** if the candidate notices problems but cannot yet clearly describe impact or a control measure.
- **Not suitable at the moment** if the candidate blindly treats data as truth and does not check whether a result may be an error.

## Role View and Work

In this group work, we looked at the Risk Analyst role from four perspectives. The hiring manager evaluated how CV, LinkedIn, and portfolio show risk thinking and precision. The technical interviewer focused on SQL, data quality checks, anomaly detection, and noticing illogical results. The HR manager evaluated responsibility, calm communication, and handling sensitive topics clearly. The team lead looked at whether the candidate's risk recommendations are practical, documented, and usable for the company.

We used the same four views in every chapter. This meant we evaluated the Risk Analyst role not only as technical checking, but also from business risk, communication, and responsibility perspectives.

## 1. Technical Skills Evaluation

In a Risk Analyst role, strong data checking, SQL, and the ability to find anomalies are expected. The candidate should be able to check duplicates, missing values, unusual amounts, and patterns.

The employer checks whether the candidate notices illogical results and does not blindly treat data as truth.

Group result: the technical test could be a dataset with hidden errors, duplicates, or unusual orders.

**Examples: what is reviewed and asked**

- Reviewed: whether the candidate finds duplicates, missing values, anomalies, illogical amounts, and checks results before concluding.
- Asked: "Which checks would you do before analysis?", "How do you separate a data error from a real risk?", "Which SQL query would you use to find duplicates?"

## 2. Portfolio Evaluation

A Risk Analyst portfolio should include a project where the candidate finds problems or risks in data. Examples include return analysis, inventory differences, payment issues, or unusual purchase patterns.

The employer checks whether the candidate can not only find a risk, but also estimate its impact and suggest a control measure.

Group result: a strong portfolio shows precision, control questions, and practical recommendations for reducing risk.

**Examples: what is reviewed and asked**

- Reviewed: whether the project shows risk detection, impact evaluation, cause investigation, and a concrete control measure.
- Asked: "What was the possible financial impact of the risk?", "How did you check that the pattern was not random?", "Which process change would you recommend?"

## 3. CV and LinkedIn Evaluation

The CV should emphasize data quality, controls, precision, reliable reports, and risk identification. Previous experience in finance, accounting, logistics, or operations is an advantage.

The LinkedIn headline could be: "Risk Analyst | SQL, Data Quality, Anomaly Detection | Reducing Business Risk with Data".

Group result: the profile must show that the candidate is detail-oriented and thinks about data reliability.

**Examples: what is reviewed and asked**

- Reviewed: whether the CV includes data quality, controls, anomalies, audit logic, finance or operations experience, and documentation.
- Asked: "Give an example where you noticed an error or risk.", "How does your previous experience help in risk analysis?", "How do you make sure a report is reliable?"

## 4. 30-60-90 Day Onboarding Plan

First 30 days: learning UrbanStyle's processes, data sources, and main risk areas.

Days 30-60: creating data quality checks and simpler risk reports.

Days 60-90: deep analysis of one risk area, such as returns or inventory differences.

Group result: a Risk Analyst must learn where the company may have the largest losses or errors.

**Examples: what is reviewed and asked**

- Reviewed: whether the plan maps main risk areas, data sources, checks, and one practical deep analysis output.
- Asked: "Which risk areas would you study first?", "Who would you meet to understand the process?", "Which control report would you create within 60 days?"

## 5. Writing a Job Offer

The job posting should emphasize data quality checks, risk identification, anomaly analysis, and collaboration with operations or finance.

Requirements: SQL, Excel, attention to detail, analytical thinking, and the ability to document results.

Group result: the posting should speak to a candidate who is precise, calm, and systematic.

**Examples: what is reviewed and asked**

- Reviewed: whether the job posting emphasizes data quality, risk identification, anomaly analysis, documentation, and collaboration with finance or operations.
- Asked: "Does the posting describe the business impact of risk?", "Do the requirements evaluate precision, not only tools?", "Is the role responsibility clear enough?"

## 6. Interview Scenario

The interview could include a case: "At UrbanStyle, the number of returns has sharply increased in one category. How would you investigate whether this is a normal change or a risk?"

The candidate should ask about data, period, category, supplier, campaigns, and customer behavior.

Group result: the Risk Analyst interview must evaluate critical thinking, precision, and the ability to offer testable next steps.

**Examples: what is reviewed and asked**

- Reviewed: whether the candidate asks about period, category, supplier, campaigns, data quality, and impact, and offers testable steps.
- Asked: "Returns increased sharply. What could be the reason?", "How do you check whether this is normal seasonality?", "Which warning or control would you create for the future?"

**More Detailed Interview Structure**

- 5 minutes: introduction and listening to the candidate's background.
- 15 minutes: portfolio or risk analysis walk-through where the candidate explains the risk found, control, and impact.
- 15 minutes: practical case, for example returns in one category grow sharply and the candidate needs to evaluate whether it is normal change or risk.
- 15 minutes: STAR method questions about precision, responsibility, sensitive topics, and documentation.
- 5 minutes: candidate questions.
- 5 minutes: explaining next steps.

**Interview Evaluation Criteria**

- Technical skill: SQL, anomaly detection, data quality checking, and result validation.
- Business thinking: whether the candidate evaluates the impact of risk, not only describes the anomaly.
- Communication: whether they can explain a sensitive risk calmly and precisely.
- Collaboration and learning ability: whether they involve process owners and offer practical control measures.

## Strong vs Weak Candidate

## Evaluation Scale Summary

| Area | 1 - needs development | 2 - suitable level | 3 - strong level |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Data checking | Does not check quality | Finds duplicates and missing values | Checks patterns from several angles |
| Risk thinking | Only describes the anomaly | Evaluates possible impact | Separates error, seasonality, and real risk |
| Technical skill | SQL or analysis is uncertain | Uses simple checks and summaries | Creates reusable control or report |
| Communication | Explains risk unclearly | Describes the problem calmly | Offers preventive action and owner |

The candidate is a strong recommendation if data checking and risk thinking are at least level 2 and the candidate offers a concrete control measure.

**Strong candidate:** is precise, critical, and checks data before making conclusions. They can find duplicates, missing values, anomalies, and illogical patterns, and evaluate the possible business impact of a risk. The portfolio includes, for example, return analysis, inventory difference analysis, or payment issue analysis with a recommended control measure.

**Weak candidate:** treats data too quickly as truth and does not check whether the result may be a data error, seasonality, or real risk. They describe a problem but do not evaluate the impact or offer preventive control. Their CV and interview lack examples of precision, responsibility, and risk thinking.
