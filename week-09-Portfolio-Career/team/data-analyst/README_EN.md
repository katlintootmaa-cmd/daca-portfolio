# Hiring Guide for the Role: Data Analyst

Estonian version: [README.md](README.md)

## Role Focus

A Data Analyst helps UrbanStyle make better decisions based on sales, customer, product, and operations data. The role focuses on data collection, cleaning, SQL queries, simpler Python analysis, dashboards, and explaining results to the business side.

## How to Use This Guide

This guide can be used throughout the Data Analyst hiring process: writing the job posting, reviewing CV and LinkedIn, evaluating the portfolio, preparing a technical test, conducting the interview, and making the final hiring decision.

For each candidate, three main questions should be evaluated:

- whether the candidate can work with data practically;
- whether they can explain results in a business context;
- whether their working style fits UrbanStyle's everyday team collaboration.

Recommended decision:

- **Move to the next round** if the candidate shows practical SQL or Python skills, can explain their portfolio, and connects results with a business question.
- **Needs additional checking** if the technical base exists, but business explanation or documentation is weak.
- **Not suitable at the moment** if the candidate only lists tools and cannot show how they would use data for decision-making.

## Role View and Work

In this group work, we looked at the Data Analyst role from four perspectives. The hiring manager considered how the role appears in the CV, LinkedIn, and job posting. The technical interviewer defined which SQL, Python, and data cleaning skills are needed. The HR manager evaluated which soft skills help a junior analyst succeed in a team. The team lead considered everyday work: how the analyst receives tasks, documents work, and explains results to others.

We used the same four views in every chapter. This meant the group work did not stay only technical, but also included candidate experience, teamwork, communication, and employer expectations.

## 1. Technical Skills Evaluation

In a Data Analyst role, a strong SQL base is expected. The candidate should be able to use SELECT, WHERE, JOIN, GROUP BY, HAVING, and CTE logic. They should also be able to clean and summarize data in Python with pandas.

The employer checks whether the candidate can answer a real business question, not only write a technically correct query. For example, a technical task could be: find UrbanStyle's most profitable customer segments and explain how they could be used in marketing.

Group result: we recommend a practical test based on sales, customer, and product data. Evaluation should look at technical accuracy, result checking, and business explanation.

**Examples: what is reviewed and asked**

- Reviewed: whether the SQL query is logical, whether JOINs do not create double-counting, whether the result is checked, and whether the conclusion answers the business question.
- Asked: "How do you check that your result is correct?", "Which table or metric would you need in addition?", "How would you explain the result to a sales manager?"

## 2. Portfolio Evaluation

A Data Analyst portfolio should include at least 2-3 projects that show data cleaning, SQL analysis, visualization, and conclusions. A good project includes a README that describes the business problem, data, tools, analysis process, and result.

The employer checks whether the candidate can move from data to a decision. A chart alone is not enough; it is necessary to explain what the chart means and what the company could do based on it.

Group result: a strong Data Analyst candidate's portfolio shows SQL, Python, dashboarding, and business interpretation as one whole.

**Examples: what is reviewed and asked**

- Reviewed: whether the project has a clear problem, data cleaning steps, visualizations, conclusions, and recommendations.
- Asked: "Why did you choose this project?", "What was the most difficult data quality problem?", "What would you improve in the project next time?"

## 3. CV and LinkedIn Evaluation

Concrete results are expected in the CV. A good example would be: "Analyzed 10,000+ sales records with SQL and Python, identifying 245 high-value customers." A weak example would be: "Worked with data."

The LinkedIn headline could be: "Junior Data Analyst | SQL, Python, Power BI | Data into Business Insights". The About section should tell the career-change story and connect previous experience with data analysis skills.

Group result: for the Data Analyst role, CV, LinkedIn, and GitHub must tell the same story: the candidate can analyze data and explain results in business terms.

**Examples: what is reviewed and asked**

- Reviewed: whether the CV has measurable results, tools, project links, and a clear connection with the data analyst role.
- Asked: "Which project in your CV best shows your analysis skills?", "How is your previous experience useful in a data role?", "Which skill are you currently developing?"

## 4. 30-60-90 Day Onboarding Plan

First 30 days: learning UrbanStyle's databases, KPIs, reports, and team. The candidate repeats existing queries and learns the business logic.

Days 30-60: smaller independent analyses, such as sales trends, category comparison, and customer segments.

Days 60-90: one complete project, such as RFM analysis or a sales dashboard with recommendations for management.

Group result: onboarding must move from learning toward creating independent value.

**Examples: what is reviewed and asked**

- Reviewed: whether the plan is realistic, connected to UrbanStyle's data and KPIs, and moves from learning to a concrete result.
- Asked: "What would you definitely want to learn in the first month?", "Which small analysis would create quick value?", "How would you ask for help if the data logic is unclear?"

## 5. Writing a Job Offer

A Data Analyst job posting must be concrete and skill-based. It should state that the role focuses on sales, customer, and product data analysis, dashboard creation, and explaining results.

Requirements: SQL, Excel, Python/pandas, visualization, GitHub or portfolio. Business thinking and the ability to explain results to non-technical people are an advantage.

Group result: the job posting should encourage career changers with a strong portfolio to apply.

**Examples: what is reviewed and asked**

- Reviewed: whether the posting describes real tasks, required tools, expected mindset, and the role of the portfolio.
- Asked: "Are the requirements realistic for a junior role?", "Does the posting separate required and useful skills?", "Which candidate does this text speak to?"

## 6. Interview Scenario

The interview should include a portfolio introduction, an SQL task, a short Python or data cleaning discussion, and STAR questions.

Example question: "How would you find UrbanStyle's most valuable customers and present the result to the marketing team?"

Group result: the Data Analyst interview must evaluate both technical skill and the ability to interpret data in business terms.

**Examples: what is reviewed and asked**

- Reviewed: whether the interview includes portfolio, SQL, data interpretation, communication, and STAR questions.
- Asked: "How would you analyze a sales decline?", "Which SQL query would you write to find customer segments?", "Tell us about a situation where you had to explain a complex result simply."

**More Detailed Interview Structure**

- 5 minutes: introduction and listening to the candidate's background.
- 15 minutes: portfolio walk-through based on one strong data analysis project.
- 15 minutes: practical SQL or Python task based on UrbanStyle's sales, customer, or product data.
- 15 minutes: STAR method behavioral questions about collaboration, time pressure, and learning ability.
- 5 minutes: candidate questions.
- 5 minutes: explaining next steps.

**Interview Evaluation Criteria**

- Technical skill: SQL, Python, data cleaning, and result checking.
- Business thinking: whether the candidate understands which decision the analysis supports.
- Communication: whether they explain results clearly to a non-technical listener.
- Collaboration and learning ability: whether they can ask for help, accept feedback, and work in a team.

## Strong vs Weak Candidate

## Evaluation Scale Summary

| Area | 1 - needs development | 2 - suitable level | 3 - strong level |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Technical skill | Knows tools only superficially | Solves a simple SQL/Python task | Checks results and justifies the solution |
| Business thinking | Does not connect analysis with a decision | Explains the basic business meaning | Offers practical recommendations for UrbanStyle |
| Portfolio | Projects are undocumented | Projects have a clear problem and result | Projects show measurable impact |
| Communication | Explanation is unclear | Explains their work understandably | Adapts the explanation to different audiences |

The candidate is a strong recommendation if most areas are at level 2 or 3 and no core area remains at level 1.

**Strong candidate:** can use SQL and Python to solve a real business question, checks result logic, and explains conclusions to a non-technical listener. Their portfolio has 2-3 clearly documented projects, such as sales data analysis, customer segmentation, or a dashboard with recommendations. In the CV and LinkedIn, skills are connected to concrete examples.

**Weak candidate:** lists tools but does not show how they used them. The portfolio has files without README, conclusions, or business explanation. In the interview, they answer technical questions superficially, do not check results, and cannot explain how analysis would support UrbanStyle's decisions.
