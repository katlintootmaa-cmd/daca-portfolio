# Week 10: Portfolio Organization and Defense

Eestikeelne versioon: [README.md](README.md)

## Goal

The goal of Week 10 was to turn 10 weeks of learning materials, analyses, dashboards, and code files into a professional GitHub portfolio. The focus was not on creating a new large analysis, but on organizing existing work, highlighting business value, and preparing for the final portfolio defense.

The portfolio needed to answer one key question:

> What did UrbanStyle actually learn from our data work, and what changed?

## Business Problem

During the program, UrbanStyle received many different types of data work: SQL queries, data cleaning scripts, Power BI dashboards, Python/Pandas analyses, and an automated API pipeline. Without a clear structure, it would be difficult for a recruiter, mentor, or board member to quickly understand which projects matter most and what impact they demonstrate.

The portfolio therefore needed to show for each project:

- what problem the project solved;
- which data, tools, and methods were used;
- what concrete result, finding, or learning outcome was produced;
- how the work creates value for UrbanStyle's decision-making.

## My Approach

I organized the portfolio from the perspective of a GitHub reader. The goal was that someone who does not know the DACA course context could understand within 30-60 seconds who I am, what I have learned, and which projects best demonstrate my data analyst skills.

During Week 10, I:

- reviewed the repository homepage and selected project descriptions;
- connected the projects more clearly to Junior Data Analyst skills;
- highlighted stronger work from SQL, Power BI, Python/Pandas, and API pipeline topics;
- checked that project READMEs included the problem, approach, result, and learning outcome;
- prepared the defense logic: what to show, which number to use, and how to explain the impact;
- added an AI usage explanation to the portfolio refinement work.

## 7-Minute Defense Structure

According to the guides, the presentation must be short, focused, and business-oriented. I used the following structure for preparation:

| Part | Time | Content |
|------|------|---------|
| Introduction | 1 min | Who we are and what our mission was for UrbanStyle |
| Data overview | 2 min | 3-5 strongest numerical findings from the full program |
| Visualizations | 2 min | Best dashboards and charts with a clear "so what?" explanation |
| Recommendations | 1 min | 3 data-driven recommendations for UrbanStyle management |
| AI usage | 1 min | How AI helped with code, READMEs, presentation structure, and review |

## My Individual 45-Second Part

My individual part focuses on presenting one strong project briefly and clearly:

- who I am and what my role was;
- which business problem the project solved;
- what the most important result or number was;
- which visual or output I can show as evidence;
- what I learned from the project.

For example, I can present either the RFM analysis or the API pipeline project, because they show both Python/Pandas skills and practical data workflow automation.

## Recommendations for the Board

In the portfolio defense, listing tools is not enough. The board needs decision-oriented recommendations. Based on my portfolio, the strongest recommendations are:

1. Focus on the most valuable customer segments, because RFM analysis helps identify which customers generate a larger share of revenue.
2. Use Power BI dashboards for regular sales and store performance tracking, because a visual overview makes trends easier to notice.
3. Automate recurring reports with a Python/API pipeline to reduce manual work and improve consistency.

## Defense Preparation

I prepared answers to questions that a mentor, board member, or employer might ask:

- why I chose a specific method or tool;
- what the data limitations were;
- how the result could be used in practice;
- what I would improve in the next version;
- how AI helped and where I had to make the final decision myself.

For answers, I use the STAR structure: situation, task, action, and result.

I used AI to audit the portfolio structure, make README wording clearer, check the defense logic, and improve some technical explanations. The final project choices, business conclusions, numbers, and personal learning reflections are based on my own work in this repository.

## Lessons Learned

Week 10 helped me understand that a portfolio is not just a collection of files. A strong portfolio tells a clear story: what problems I solved, which tools I used, what the results were, and how the work helps people make better decisions.

The most important lesson was the "so what?" test. If a project description does not explain why the work matters, the wording needs to be improved. A portfolio should show not only learning, but also value.
